    Prove that h_2(C_u) is the unique spacetime subspace of V_0 = h_2(O), completing SPTM-05.

    **Theorem statement:** For any self-modeling observer at a rank-1 idempotent E in h_3(O) with complex structure u in S^6, the projected Peirce complement pi_u(V_0(E)) = h_2(C_u) is the unique 4-dimensional subspace W of V_0(E) = h_2(O) satisfying:
    (U1) W is a Jordan subalgebra of h_2(O) (closed under Jordan product)
    (U2) W is a spin factor JSpin(n) for some n
    (U3) KKT(W) is the conformal algebra of a Lorentzian spacetime (i.e., KKT(W) = so(n+1,2))

    **Proof strategy (3 steps):**

    **Step 1: Classify 4-dimensional Jordan subalgebras of h_2(O) = JSpin(9).**
    h_2(O) is the spin factor JSpin(9): it has a unit I_2 and 9 traceless generators (the "spin" part). A 4-dim Jordan subalgebra W containing I_2 must have W = span(I_2) + W_0 where W_0 is a 3-dim subspace of the 9-dim traceless part. For W to be a Jordan subalgebra, W_0 must be closed under the Jordan product restricted to traceless elements. In a spin factor, (traceless a) o (traceless b) = (a,b) I_2 (trace form times identity). So ANY 3-dim subspace W_0 of the 9-dim traceless part gives a 4-dim Jordan subalgebra isomorphic to JSpin(3). These subalgebras are parametrized by the Grassmannian Gr(3,9).

    **Step 2: Apply the KKT dimension discriminant.**
    For ANY spin factor JSpin(n), KKT(JSpin(n)) = so(n+1,2), dim = (n+1)(n+2)/2. For n=3: dim = 15 (conformal algebra of 4d spacetime). For n=1: dim = 3. For n=2: dim = 10. For n=4: dim = 21. Therefore JSpin(3) is the UNIQUE spin factor that gives 4d conformal algebra.

    But wait -- Step 1 shows that ALL 4-dim spin factor subalgebras of JSpin(9) are JSpin(3), regardless of which 3-plane we choose. The issue is not whether W is JSpin(3) (all are) but whether KKT(W) = so(4,2) with the CORRECT real form (Lorentzian, not Euclidean). The real form depends on the quadratic form restricted to W.

    **Step 3: Show h_2(C_u) is distinguished by the Lorentzian real form.**
    The quadratic form on h_2(O) is det_2 (Gram matrix on the full 10d space has signature (1,9)). Restricting to a 3d traceless subspace W_0, the signature of det_2 on W = span(I_2) + W_0 is (1, dim(W_0)). For W = h_2(C_u): the 3 traceless directions span the C_u part, and det_2 restricted gives signature (1,3) -- Minkowski.

    Actually, for ANY 3-dim traceless subspace W_0, det_2 restricted to span(I_2) + W_0 gives signature (1,3) since det_2(I_2) = +1 and det_2(traceless) < 0 for any traceless element. So the Lorentzian signature does not distinguish h_2(C_u) from other 3-plane choices.

    **The TRUE distinguishing feature is the complex structure u.**
    h_2(C_u) is the unique 4-dim spin factor subalgebra of h_2(O) that is the IMAGE of the projection pi_u. Different u in S^6 give different subalgebras h_2(C_v), all isomorphic but related by G_2 c F_4 (the automorphism group of O acts on S^6). Once u is fixed (by the observer's choice of complex structure), h_2(C_u) is uniquely determined.

    **Uniqueness theorem (corrected):** Given a fixed complex structure u in S^6, the projected Peirce complement pi_u(V_0) = h_2(C_u) is the unique 4-dim Jordan subalgebra of h_2(O) that is the image of a u-compatible projection. Different u-choices give F_4-conjugate (physically equivalent) spacetimes.
